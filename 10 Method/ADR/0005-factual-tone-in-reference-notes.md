# 0005: Factual tone in reference notes, and its exceptions

**Status:** Accepted · Decided 2026-06-15, recorded 2026-08-06

## Context

Most of this vault is not the book. It is the material the book is written from:
how the Noetic Force behaves, what the twelve peoples are, what happened when.
Those notes get read while writing a scene, and what is needed then is the fact,
quickly.

Notes drift the other way on their own. A worldbuilding note is written in the
same sitting as the idea that excites its author, and the excitement gets into
the prose: a heading becomes a subtitle, a mechanic becomes an image, a
limitation becomes a warning. The result reads well once and badly every time
after, because the fact is now inside a sentence built to land rather than to
inform.

The tone pass of 2026-06-15 went through `02` to `06` and removed exactly that.
This record holds what the pass established, because the pull that produced the
drift has not gone away.

## Decision

**Reference notes read like a neutral knowledge base.** This governs headings and
body text.

**Headings** are short, neutral labels. Not `Begriff — eine poetische Pointe`, no
rhetorical questions, no dramatic colons.

**Body text** states facts and mechanics plainly. Out: dramatizing flourishes
(*„treibt unaufhaltsam auf die eigene Vernichtung zu"*), evaluative or aphoristic
phrasing, punchy one-line antitheses, and chatty fillers (*„ehrlich"*,
*„Pikant:"*, *„die Krönung"*).

**No authorial *wir/uns* framing.** *„In dieser Note beschreiben wir …"* becomes
*„Diese Notiz beschreibt …"*. The note describes the world; it does not narrate
its own writing.

**Six things stay subjective by design**, and they are the whole exception list:

1. the German story prose in `08 Writing` — that is the product;
2. sanctioned author-voice blocks, `> *Kritische Anmerkung:*` and
   `> *Anmerkung:*`;
3. in-world quotations;
4. spoiler warnings;
5. `Merksatz` axioms;
6. the craft framing in `05 Story Architecture` and `09 Meta`, but only where it
   talks about *writing* the story rather than about facts of the world.

Exception 6 carries the boundary that matters: the same note can hold both. A
paragraph about how a reveal should be paced is craft; a paragraph about what the
reveal *is* is a world fact and takes the neutral tone.

## Consequences

**This is the only convention in the vault that no mechanical test could ever
approach.** Tone is read, not counted. `#wip` versus `#ready` can at least be
compared against a section list; „liest sich wie ein Essay" cannot. It survives
on being written down and on being noticed in review, which is why the exception
list is exhaustive rather than illustrative — an open-ended list of exceptions is
permission.

**Applying it to an existing note is a rewrite, not a pass.** The 2026-06-15
sweep touched headings and body across `02` to `06`. Anything written before that
and not touched by it may still carry the old register, and nothing marks which
notes were reviewed.

**It costs something real.** A neutral sentence is less memorable than a vivid
one, and some of what the sweep removed was good writing. That is accepted: the
vault is read for reference, and the vivid version of a mechanic is what belongs
in the chapter that uses it.

**The author-voice block is the pressure valve.** An observation that is a
judgment rather than a fact has a place to go — `> *Kritische Anmerkung:*` —
which is what keeps the rule from squeezing genuine editorial thinking out of the
vault entirely.

## Alternatives considered

**One tone everywhere, prose included.** Rejected: it is not a style rule, it is a
rule about what a document is for. `08 Writing` is the novel.

**No rule; let each note find its register.** Rejected on evidence. That was the
state before 2026-06-15, and the sweep found essayistic headings and dramatizing
body text across five top-level folders. The pull is consistent enough that
leaving it unstated means it returns.

**An illustrative exception list, extended when a case comes up.** Rejected: an
exception list that grows on demand is not a list, it is a habit of granting
exceptions. Six is what the vault actually needs; a seventh is a change to this
record.
